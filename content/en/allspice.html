Allspice

Pimenta dioica

Allspice is native to the forests that run from southern Mexico through Central America. In northern Alta Verapaz it grows as a tall tree in home gardens and agroforestry plots, and families harvest its berries once a year.

{{icon:allspice}}

One harvest a year

The berries are picked in July and August, when they are fully grown but still green, and dried in the sun until they turn deep brown and develop their aroma of clove, cinnamon and pepper.

Because the whole year's supply comes in two months, we agree volumes with producer organizations before the harvest and plan collection and storage with them, so families are not forced to sell in a hurry at a low price.

The trees are left standing for decades. Every allspice harvest is income that comes from keeping trees, not cutting them.

Common uses

  • Baking: pies, gingerbread and spiced cakes
  • Spice blends and jerk seasoning
  • Meat processing and sausages
  • Pickling and preserves
  • Pimento oil extraction
