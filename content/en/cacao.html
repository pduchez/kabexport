Cacao that keeps the rainforest standing

The Lachuá Ecoregion lies in the northern lowlands of Alta Verapaz, between the Chixoy River and Laguna Lachuá. Its 53,523 hectares, the national park and its buffer zone, are a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance and home to Q’eqchi’ Maya farming communities.

Here cacao grows under shade trees, in agroforestry systems: about 450 hectares have been planted in areas set aside for forest restoration. For these families it is one of the few crops that earns a living and keeps the land covered at the same time.

Our current lots come from a Q’eqchi’ family enterprise in Lachuá that grows, ferments and exports fine cacao. We also work with cacao organizations in Raxruhá.

Two fine-flavour genetic groups

  • Criollo: the heirloom cacao of the Maya lowlands, with a fine, delicate flavour.
  • Trinitario: Criollo crossed with Forastero, fine flavour with vigour and yield.

Common uses

  • Bean-to-bar and craft chocolate
  • Couverture
  • Cocoa nibs and drinking chocolate
  • Confectionery and bakery

Post-harvest protocol

Quality is made after the harvest

Fermentation is where cacao flavour is formed. A controlled, repeatable process is what lets a chocolate maker get the same bean, lot after lot.

Harvest

Ripe pods, picked by hand from shade-grown trees.

Controlled fermentation

Under a defined protocol for time and turning, so flavour develops the same way every batch.

Drying

Slow drying down to export moisture, so the beans keep in transit and in your warehouse.

Selection and lot record

Sorted and checked by cut test. Every lot keeps its identity from origin to your door.
