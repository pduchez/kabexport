Cinnamon

Cinnamomum

Cinnamon is the inner bark of a tree. Community growers in northern Alta Verapaz cultivate it in their plots and peel it by hand, a skill passed on within families.

Bark that rolls itself

Young shoots are cut, the outer bark is scraped off and the inner bark is peeled in strips. As it dries in the shade, it curls into quills.

The trees regrow from the base after cutting, so the same plant keeps producing for years.

Cinnamon gives families income at a different time of year from cardamom and allspice, which spreads their earnings across the calendar.

Common uses

  • Baking and pastry
  • Coffee, tea and hot drinks
  • Spice blends
  • Confectionery and cereals
  • Mexican and Central American cooking
