Our producers

The producers are the reason this company exists.

For KAB EXPORT, the producer chains of Alta Verapaz and the social and environmental sustainability of their communities matter more than any commercial gain. Every decision we make starts there.

Where we work

Northern Alta Verapaz, Guatemala

We work in the lowlands of the north of the department, with family farms, cooperatives and community associations, many of them in Q'eqchi' Maya communities.

Chisec

Family farms and community associations that grow cardamom, allspice, cinnamon and cacao. Among them are family growers with certified organic cardamom.

Lachuá region

Along the Chixoy river, producer families specialized in fine-flavour cacao, who ferment and dry their own beans and those they collect from neighbours.

Raxruhá

Producer associations that grow cacao and allspice, and an organization that collects and processes fermented cacao, allspice and cardamom in its own facility.

How we work with them

Our commitments to producers

Clear prices, agreed in advance

Producers know what they will be paid before they harvest, and are paid on time on the terms agreed.

Through their organizations

We buy through cooperatives and community associations, and help them grow stronger instead of going around them.

Value stays at origin

Drying, fermenting, sorting and packing are done in Alta Verapaz, by the producers or close to them.

The long term

We plan volumes season after season, so families can count on a buyer and plan their own year.

Why it matters

Income that keeps families on their land

In northern Alta Verapaz, spices and cacao are an important source of cash income for rural families. When prices are unclear, or when families have to sell in a hurry to whoever arrives first, most of the value is lost before the product leaves the community.

A buyer that pays fairly, plans ahead and respects the producers' own organizations changes that. That is the role we want KAB EXPORT to play.

Growing several crops among trees, instead of one crop in the open, also protects families from the price swings of any single product.
